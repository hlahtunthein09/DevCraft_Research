# DevCraft Financial Model — With Rationale and Presenter Explanation

## Purpose

This document explains **where each number comes from, why that number was chosen, how it is calculated, and how to explain it in front of the class**.

The numbers below are **classroom planning assumptions for a lean MVP**, not proven real-world DevCraft operating data.

The presentation should always distinguish:

- **Assumption** = a number we choose for planning
- **Calculation** = a number produced from the assumption
- **Interpretation** = what the result means for the business

---

# 1. Business Stage Assumption

DevCraft is presented as a **founder-run MVP / early launch digital platform**.

That means:

- founders build the product themselves
- founders handle most product decisions
- there is no full-time sales team yet
- there is no dedicated HR department
- there is no office rent
- there is no full-time moderation team yet
- there is no large marketing department yet

Therefore, the financial model should show **minimum monthly cash operating cost**, not the cost of a mature company.

This is why the fixed cost is deliberately small.

---

# 2. Pricing Assumptions

## Free — ฿0

Why free?

DevCraft needs a large community before premium features become valuable.

Free users:

- create posts
- ask questions
- share projects
- join basic challenges
- build public profiles
- discover jobs

Free users do not directly create subscription revenue, but they make the platform useful.

Presenter explanation:

> “We keep the basic community free because DevCraft depends on network activity. If every user had to pay from the beginning, it would be harder to build the community.”

---

## Developer Pro — ฿199/month

Why ฿199?

This is a **low-entry consumer subscription assumption**.

The target user includes:

- students
- junior developers
- learners
- early-career developers

฿199 is intentionally positioned as affordable enough for individual users while still creating recurring revenue.

What Pro could include:

- premium courses
- advanced challenges
- better portfolio tools
- learning analytics
- advanced saved-learning features

Presenter explanation:

> “Developer Pro is priced lower because the customer is an individual learner. We want a price that is easier to accept for students and junior developers.”

Important:
This is a proposed classroom price, not a validated market price.

---

## Creator Pro — ฿299/month

Why higher than Developer Pro?

A creator receives business tools, not only learning features.

Possible value:

- course publishing tools
- creator analytics
- audience insights
- promotional tools
- marketplace benefits

Presenter explanation:

> “Creators can earn money through DevCraft, so the creator plan can be slightly higher than the learner plan because it supports income-generating activity.”

---

## Company Starter — ฿1,490/month

Why much higher?

A company uses DevCraft for recruitment.

The economic value of:

- finding candidates
- publishing jobs
- viewing portfolios
- running hiring challenges

is higher than the value of one individual learning subscription.

Presenter explanation:

> “A company is not buying a course. It is buying hiring access and recruitment tools, so the value per customer is much higher.”

---

## Company Pro — ฿3,990/month

Why?

This is intended for companies with more frequent recruitment needs.

Possible differences:

- more active job posts
- advanced filtering
- candidate comparison
- recruiter analytics
- featured employer branding
- advanced hiring challenge tools

Presenter explanation:

> “The Pro company plan assumes a larger company gains more value because it recruits more often and uses more advanced tools.”

---

# 3. Base User Assumptions

## Community MAU = 5,000

Why 5,000?

This is not a measured forecast.

It is chosen as a **classroom base-case scale** large enough to show that:

- a community exists
- subscriptions can generate meaningful revenue
- creator activity can happen
- employer activity can be supported

It is also small enough to represent an early digital platform rather than a giant social network.

Presenter explanation:

> “We use 5,000 monthly active users as a base-case assumption. It is not a forecast. It gives us a practical number for testing whether the business model can work financially.”

---

## Developer Pro = 450 users

Calculation:

450 ÷ 5,000 = 9%

Why 450?

We assume approximately 9% of the community chooses Developer Pro.

This is a planning assumption used to test the model.

Presenter explanation:

> “Out of 5,000 active community users, we assume 450 become Developer Pro users. That is a 9% paid conversion assumption.”

Do not say:
“9% conversion is proven.”

Say:
“9% is our base-case assumption.”

---

## Creator Pro = 120 users

Calculation:

120 ÷ 5,000 = 2.4%

Why 120?

Not every user creates courses or challenges.

The creator group should be much smaller than the learner group.

Presenter explanation:

> “Creators are a smaller segment, so we assume only 120 out of 5,000 active users pay for Creator Pro.”

---

## Free users

5,000 − 450 − 120

= 4,430 free users

This means:

- 4,430 free
- 450 Developer Pro
- 120 Creator Pro

Total:

5,000 community MAU

Paid individual/creator conversion:

570 ÷ 5,000

= 11.4%

Presenter explanation:

> “The majority of users remain free. Only 11.4% of the community pays for either Developer Pro or Creator Pro in our base case.”

---

# 4. Company Assumptions

Companies are modeled separately from community MAU.

## Company Starter = 25

## Company Pro = 5

Total paying companies:

30

Why 30?

This is a base-case B2B assumption.

The idea is that a platform with 5,000 active developers could potentially be useful to a small number of employers.

Presenter explanation:

> “We keep the employer side small. We assume 30 paying companies in total: 25 Starter and 5 Pro.”

Again:
This is a classroom planning assumption.

---

# 5. Subscription Revenue

## Developer Pro

450 users × ฿199

= **฿89,550**

Where does it come from?

Number of paying Developer Pro users × monthly price.

Presenter explanation:

> “Developer Pro contributes ฿89,550 because 450 users each pay ฿199 per month.”

---

## Creator Pro

120 creators × ฿299

= **฿35,880**

Presenter explanation:

> “Creator Pro contributes ฿35,880 because 120 creators each pay ฿299 per month.”

---

## Company Starter

25 companies × ฿1,490

= **฿37,250**

---

## Company Pro

5 companies × ฿3,990

= **฿19,950**

---

# 6. Marketplace Revenue

Assumption:

250 paid course purchases per month

Average course price:

฿399

Why 250 purchases?

This is a transaction-volume assumption.

With:

- 5,000 active community users
- 120 active creators

250 monthly paid purchases means the marketplace is active but not extremely large.

Presenter explanation:

> “We assume 250 paid course purchases per month. This is not 250 different users necessarily; one user could buy more than one course.”

---

## Marketplace GMV

250 × ฿399

= **฿99,750**

GMV means total value of course transactions.

But DevCraft does not keep all ฿99,750.

---

## Platform commission = 20%

Why 20%?

This is a proposed marketplace commission assumption.

Creator receives:

80%

DevCraft receives:

20%

DevCraft commission:

฿99,750 × 20%

= **฿19,950**

Creator payout:

฿99,750 × 80%

= **฿79,800**

Presenter explanation:

> “The marketplace generates ฿99,750 in total transaction value, but DevCraft only counts the 20% platform commission as platform revenue.”

This is important.

Do not call GMV DevCraft revenue.

---

# 7. Affiliate Revenue

Assumption:

100 successful referrals per month

Average affiliate commission:

฿120

Calculation:

100 × ฿120

= **฿12,000**

Why use this?

DevCraft can recommend products relevant to developers, such as:

- hosting
- developer tools
- domains
- certification
- technical learning products

Presenter explanation:

> “We assume 100 successful affiliate conversions, with an average commission of ฿120 each. This gives ฿12,000 monthly affiliate revenue.”

Important:

This is an assumption.

If the teacher asks:

“Why ฿120?”

Answer:

> “It is not a claimed universal affiliate rate. We use it as a blended planning assumption for the classroom model.”

---

# 8. Sponsorship Revenue

Assumption:

1 small sponsored campaign per month

Price:

฿15,000

Revenue:

**฿15,000**

Possible sponsorship examples:

- sponsored coding challenge
- sponsored learning week
- featured technology partner
- branded developer event

Presenter explanation:

> “We include only one small sponsored campaign per month in the base case instead of assuming many advertisers.”

Why only one?

To keep the model conservative and simple.

---

# 9. Promoted Jobs

Assumption:

10 promoted job posts per month

Promotion fee:

฿490 each

Calculation:

10 × ฿490

= **฿4,900**

Presenter explanation:

> “Companies can optionally pay to increase the visibility of a job post. We assume only 10 promoted jobs per month.”

---

# 10. Total Monthly Revenue

Now add every revenue source.

Developer Pro:

฿89,550

Creator Pro:

฿35,880

Company Starter:

฿37,250

Company Pro:

฿19,950

Marketplace commission:

฿19,950

Affiliate:

฿12,000

Sponsorship:

฿15,000

Promoted jobs:

฿4,900

Calculation:

89,550
+ 35,880
+ 37,250
+ 19,950
+ 19,950
+ 12,000
+ 15,000
+ 4,900

= **฿234,480**

Presenter explanation:

> “Our base-case monthly revenue is ฿234,480. The largest source is Developer Pro, but the important point is that DevCraft does not depend on only one revenue stream.”

# 11. Fixed Cost

## Fixed cost means:

A cost we must pay to operate the MVP even if one extra user does not join.

For the lean MVP we assume:

---

## Hosting / database = ฿4,000

Why?

DevCraft needs:

- application hosting
- database
- file storage
- backups
- basic server resources

This is a planning allowance, not a quoted invoice.

Presenter explanation:

> “We reserve ฿4,000 per month for the platform's minimum hosting and database infrastructure.”

---

## Essential software / email / monitoring = ฿2,000

Possible tools:

- domain
- business email
- monitoring
- analytics
- developer tools

Presenter explanation:

> “We include ฿2,000 for small recurring software and monitoring services.”

---

## Marketing = ฿8,000

Why?

Even a founder-run MVP still needs some money for:

- small social media ads
- challenge promotion
- university community campaigns
- launch content

Presenter explanation:

> “We deliberately keep marketing small at ฿8,000 because this is an MVP, not a large growth campaign.”

---

## Admin / accounting = ฿2,000

This is a small allowance for:

- accounting
- basic administration
- small business expenses

---

## Contingency = ฿4,000

Why?

Real businesses face small unexpected expenses.

Examples:

- additional service usage
- software upgrade
- small one-off operating expense

Presenter explanation:

> “We keep a ฿4,000 contingency so the model does not assume everything goes perfectly every month.”

---

## Total Fixed Cost

฿4,000 hosting
+ ฿2,000 tools
+ ฿8,000 marketing
+ ฿2,000 admin
+ ฿4,000 contingency

= **฿20,000/month**

---

## Important limitation

Founder salaries are not included.

Why?

Because the model represents a founder-run MVP.

Presenter explanation:

> “The ฿20,000 is cash operating overhead. It does not mean running DevCraft truly costs only ฿20,000 forever. Founder labor is excluded at the MVP stage.”

This sentence is important.

---

# 12. Variable Cost

## Variable cost means:

Costs that grow as payments and usage grow.

We use cost categories instead of mysterious per-user numbers.

---

## Payment / transaction fees = ฿7,000

Why?

Every paid subscription and marketplace transaction can create payment-processing costs.

Instead of applying a complicated payment fee to every transaction type, the classroom model uses one blended monthly allowance:

**฿7,000**

Presenter explanation:

> “Payment fees grow with paid transactions, so we treat them as variable cost. We use ฿7,000 as a blended monthly allowance.”

---

## Extra bandwidth / storage / DB usage = ฿2,500

Why?

The ฿4,000 fixed hosting budget covers the minimum platform.

But more active users create extra:

- storage
- database requests
- bandwidth
- file delivery

We assume:

**฿2,500**

for usage above the base allowance.

---

## Email / notifications / media delivery = ฿1,500

Why?

More users create more:

- transactional email
- notifications
- course content delivery
- media traffic

Assumption:

**฿1,500**

---

## Refund / transaction reserve = ฿1,000

Why?

Some transactions may create:

- refunds
- payment issues
- small marketplace transaction costs

We keep:

**฿1,000**

as a variable reserve.

---

## Total Variable Cost

฿7,000
+ ฿2,500
+ ฿1,500
+ ฿1,000

= **฿12,000**

Presenter explanation:

> “The variable cost is small because DevCraft sells digital access, not physical products. There is no packaging, inventory, or delivery cost for every user.”

---

# 13. Contribution Margin

Contribution tells us how much revenue remains after variable costs.

Formula:

Contribution = Revenue − Variable Cost

Calculation:

฿234,480 − ฿12,000

= **฿222,480**

This money must still pay fixed cost.

---

## Contribution Margin Ratio

Formula:

Contribution ÷ Revenue

฿222,480 ÷ ฿234,480

= **94.9%**

How to explain:

> “This does not mean 94.9% profit. It means after variable costs, 94.9% of revenue is still available to cover fixed costs and then profit.”

This distinction is very important.

---

# 14. Profit

Revenue:

฿234,480

Minus variable cost:

− ฿12,000

Contribution:

฿222,480

Minus fixed cost:

− ฿20,000

MVP operating profit:

= **฿202,480**

---

## Operating Margin

Formula:

Profit ÷ Revenue

฿202,480 ÷ ฿234,480

= **86.4%**

How to explain this high margin:

> “The margin is high because this is a founder-run digital MVP and founder salaries are excluded. It should not be interpreted as the long-term net margin of a mature company.”

Use the label:

**MVP operating profit before founder salaries and tax**

This is more accurate than simply saying “net profit”.

---

# 15. Break-Even Analysis

Break-even means:

Revenue equals total cost.

The business makes:

- no profit
- no loss

The class BEP formula uses contribution margin.

Formula:

Break-Even Revenue = Fixed Cost ÷ Contribution Margin Ratio

Fixed cost:

฿20,000

Contribution margin ratio:

0.949

Calculation:

฿20,000 ÷ 0.949

≈ **฿21,075**

Round for presentation:

# ≈ ฿21,000 monthly revenue

Meaning:

> “Once DevCraft generates around ฿21,000 per month under this lean MVP cost structure, it can cover its monthly cash operating costs.”

---

# 16. Why Base Revenue Is Much Higher Than Break-Even

Base revenue:

฿234,480

Break-even:

≈ ฿21,000

Why such a big difference?

Because the model assumes:

- founders take no salary
- no office
- no full-time staff
- very small marketing
- digital delivery
- low physical operating cost

Presenter explanation:

> “This is why the MVP break-even is low. If DevCraft later hires full-time employees, the fixed cost and break-even point will increase.”

---

# 17. What Happens Later?

The current financial model represents only the MVP stage.

When DevCraft grows, costs will rise.

Possible future costs:

- full-time software engineers
- community moderators
- creator success team
- sales / business development
- customer support
- larger marketing budget
- legal and compliance
- larger cloud infrastructure

Therefore:

**MVP Model ≠ Mature Company Model**

Presenter explanation:

> “Our first goal is to validate the business with low fixed cost. If revenue grows, we can then use part of the profit to hire staff and scale the company.”

---

# 18. One-Minute Financial Explanation for Presentation

You can explain the full model like this:

> “For our base case, we assume DevCraft reaches 5,000 monthly active community users. Most users remain free. We assume 450 users subscribe to Developer Pro and 120 subscribe to Creator Pro. On the business side, we assume 25 Company Starter customers and 5 Company Pro customers.
>
> These subscriptions, together with marketplace commission, affiliate revenue, one sponsorship campaign, and promoted job posts, create total monthly revenue of ฿234,480.
>
> Because DevCraft is a digital MVP, we keep the fixed monthly cash cost low. We estimate ฿20,000 for hosting, software tools, small marketing, administration, and contingency. Variable costs such as payment fees, additional cloud usage, notifications, and transaction reserves are estimated at ฿12,000.
>
> After variable costs, the contribution is ฿222,480. After fixed costs, the MVP operating profit is approximately ฿202,480 before founder salaries and tax.
>
> The contribution margin ratio is about 94.9%, so with ฿20,000 fixed cost, break-even revenue is approximately ฿21,000 per month.
>
> This does not mean DevCraft will permanently have an 86% profit margin. The margin is high because this is a founder-run MVP. As the company grows and hires employees, fixed costs and break-even will increase.”

# 19. Questions the Teacher May Ask

## “Why is fixed cost only ฿20,000?”

Answer:

> “Because this is a founder-run MVP model. We do not include founder salaries, full-time staff, or office rent yet. The ฿20,000 represents minimum monthly cash operating overhead.”

---

## “Why is variable cost only ฿12,000?”

Answer:

> “DevCraft delivers digital services. It has no physical inventory, packaging, or delivery cost per user. The main variable costs are payment processing and usage-dependent infrastructure.”

---

## “Why is the profit margin so high?”

Answer:

> “Because the model excludes founder salaries and represents an MVP. It is operating profit before founder salaries and tax, not a mature-company net margin.”

---

## “Where does ฿234,480 revenue come from?”

Answer:

> “It is the sum of Developer Pro, Creator Pro, Company Starter, Company Pro, marketplace commission, affiliate revenue, sponsorship, and promoted jobs. Every revenue stream is calculated separately.”

---

## “Why do you use 5,000 users?”

Answer:

> “It is a classroom base-case assumption used to test the business model. We are not claiming DevCraft already has 5,000 users.”

---

## “Are the prices validated?”

Answer:

> “No. They are proposed pricing assumptions for the business model. Real launch pricing would require customer research and testing.”

---

## “What is the difference between contribution and profit?”

Answer:

> “Contribution is revenue after variable cost. Profit is what remains after both variable and fixed costs are paid.”

---

## “Why is marketplace GMV not counted as full revenue?”

Answer:

> “Because most of the course payment belongs to the creator. DevCraft only counts its platform commission as its own revenue.”

# 20. Presentation Rule

Never show a number alone.

For every important number, show:

**Assumption → Formula → Result → Meaning**

Example:

```text
450 Developer Pro users
× ฿199/month
= ฿89,550 revenue

Why?
We assume 9% of 5,000 MAU upgrades to Developer Pro.
```

That is the standard for every financial section.
